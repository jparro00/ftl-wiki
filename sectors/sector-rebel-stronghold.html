Sector profile · REBEL_SECTOR_MINIBOSS

Rebel Stronghold

A Rebel sector with one extra beacon, and that beacon is a second Flagship sitting half-built in its own shipyard. Killing the unfinished hull pays a weapon, 5 fuel, 5 missiles, 5 drone parts, two jumps of breathing room and a ship unlock, for a fight against 10 hull, shields running at 2 power of a possible 8, and no weapons at all. Everything else is the ordinary Rebel pool: six to eight beacons that open shooting, one guaranteed boarding, and seven events that can put the fleet a jump closer.

Solid blocks are the minimum the sector must place; faded blocks are the optional extra. Open a line to see every event it can place; open an event to read its card here.

Lines are filled in this order, and the map holds at most 24 beacons (19–24 by the community wiki's count). When they run out, generation stops — a line near the bottom can receive nothing at all.

This sector allocates up to 31 slots against 24 beacons, so the bottom of the table is a wish list rather than a promise.

Could be cut entirely if everything above rolls high: NEUTRAL_REBEL.

Placed before everything else, whatever the file order, because the cloud graphics come first: NEBULA_REBEL. A cloud drawn over an ordinary beacon converts it, and that beacon draws from the shared NEBULA list instead.

Beacons still empty at the end of the table are filled from the shared NEUTRAL list (OVERRIDE_NEUTRAL under Advanced Edition).

The exit beacon is not in this table at all — it draws from the shared EXIT_LIST, and an exit inside a cloud is always empty.

Entry beacon is always START_BEACON_REBEL.

Versus a plain Rebel Controlled Sector: one row. The same nine lists, the same minimums and maximums, the same start beacon — FLAGSHIP_CONSTRUCTION at 1–1 is the whole difference, and it is what makes this sector unique and keeps it out of the early game. The nebula line is the reason the spread looks so wide, and it is also the line that decides how much room the rest of the table gets: it is drawn before any of them, and it can come out at nothing or at a fifth of the map.

Blue options that pay here

  • Cloaking ×4The sector's best key: past the guard into the cache at Auto-ship near storage station and, graded across two levels, at Auto-ship near storage station in nebula; out of the storm fight at Auto-ship fight in plasma storm; and never seen at all at Rebel fight choice in nebula.
  • Crew Teleporter ×5Beams onto the station at Auto-ship near sensor station for the map data with no fight, and takes the survivors straight off Nebula lost ship — a crew member and scrap with no roll at all. Also opens Asteroid belt distress, a combat-trained rescue at Rebel ship attacking Federation loyalists, and the way to pull your own people back out of Merchant's request.
  • Sensors · ScannersSensors 3 reads the station remotely at Auto-ship near sensor station and scans Deactivated Auto-ship before you wake it up — the plain download can turn a derelict scout into a live one. Sensors 2 or 3 and Long-Ranged Scanners each buy a second pass at the hidden Federation base, whichever event sent you there.
  • Hacking ×2At Auto-ship near radar station one drone part buys the map and a jump of fleet delay outright, where the manual hack can just as easily trip the alarm and cost you one. Graded 1 and 2 into the cache at Auto-ship near storage station in nebula.
  • Engines · PilotingEngines 3 or 6 to outrun Auto-ship fight in plasma storm, Engines 4 to break contact once spotted at Rebel fight choice in nebula. Piloting 2 is the clean way through Plasma storm incapacitated ships — the gated outcomes contain no crew loss, no hull damage and no breach.
  • One-offsA Rockman or a repair drone walks into the fire at Fire on research station — both skip the roll that kills someone, and pay an augment or a drone. Engi Med-bot Dispersal or a Healing Burst at Rebel ship attacking Federation loyalists; Adv. FTL Navigation at Escort civilians FTL haywire; a defence drone, repair drone or Rock Plating at Asteroid belt distress; any missile weapon at Asteroid mining colony.

What you can leave with

  • Nothing namedNot one named weapon, drone or augment drops anywhere in this pool, and the sector declares no store rarity list of its own — every item here is a random draw or a store shelf.
  • CrewTwenty-three of the fifty events can end with someone new aboard, many of them through dead-crew salvage rather than a choice you make. Crew hiring station is the only one that simply sells you one, and Fire on research station is the only one that hands over a survivor with a name.
  • The hidden Federation baseThree separate events plant the same marker — Asteroid belt distress, Rebel ship attacking Federation loyalists and Encrypted federation signal — and one of the outcomes waiting there repairs 35 hull. Sensors 2 or 3, or Long-Ranged Scanners, buy a second scan pass on top.
  • StoresOne store guaranteed, two at most, in a sector whose set-piece is a Flagship — this is a thin place to be counting on repairs. The item pool trades on top: Trade resources in nebula and Trade resources swap fuel, missiles and drone parts in both directions, Sell missiles for scrap and Sell drone parts for scrap turn ammunition back into scrap, and Escort civilians FTL haywire can finish with a shop on a distress beacon.
  • Six quest startsThe most of anything this sector hands out. Besides the three Federation-base routes: Merchant's request (two different jobs), Escort civilians FTL haywire, and the defector's scrap cache at Rebel defector.
  • Map dataDeactivated Auto-ship, Auto-ship near sensor station, Auto-ship near radar station and Rebel transport ship can all reveal the sector map — useful here precisely because the shipyard beacon is worth routing to.

What can bite

  • The fleet clockSeven events can double pursuit for a jump; only two can push it back, Auto-ship near radar station by one and the shipyard by two. Note what triggers it: scouts escaping at Auto-ship warning and Rebel ship warning, losing your bearings at Rebel fight choice in nebula, and abandoning the family in the asteroid field at Asteroid belt distress.
  • Ten forced fightsTen events in the pool start shooting on arrival, and the hostile entry alone is 6–8 beacons — the largest allocation on the page. Another eighteen events can end in combat if you push them.
  • Boarders ×6Everything that boards you here is human. Boarders: rebels in nebula puts 3–4 aboard with the nebula blinding your sensors, Boarders: Humans near sun 2–4 with solar flares landing on top, and neither offers a choice before it happens.
  • Crew loss ×4Fire on research station and Plasma storm incapacitated ships can each kill someone outright, and both have a blue option that skips the risk entirely. The other two, Rebel defector and Merchant's request, both lose the crew member to someone you took aboard on purpose.
  • The defectorSix written outcomes once you take him aboard: three are the crew member you were promised, one gutted engines and a jump of pursuit, one two more boarders and a hole in the hull, one a dead crew member. Rejecting him is not free either — one of those outcomes leaves him loose on your ship as an intruder.
  • The station guardsAuto-ship near storage station, Auto-ship near sensor station and Auto-ship near radar station all look optional and all turn hostile if the sneaky route rolls badly. Without the matching system the only ways in are a fight or a shrug.

Reading the map here

  • The shipyard hides in plain sightNothing labels it: no store icon, no distress icon, and Long-Ranged Scanners report no ship there. The one beacon worth planning a route around is the one the map refuses to point at, so plan the route from the sector's shape rather than from its markers.
  • The red edge deletes contentA beacon the fleet takes is rewritten — the event that was waiting there and any hazard with it, replaced by an Elite Fighter that pays 1 fuel and no scrap, and outside the cloud by an anti-ship battery firing 3 damage and a breach. Everything in these pools is only on offer until the shading arrives, the shipyard included.
  • Clouds are the brakeSitting in a cloud halves the fleet's advance for that jump here, which is the only repeatable delay the sector sells — but sensors go dark inside, and a cloud beacon the fleet has already taken always comes with an ion storm on top.
  • Quests late are quests lostA marker planted with few jumps left is pushed into the next sector rather than this one, it will not be placed inside the cloud, and if this sector is your seventh the pushed quest is cancelled instead — no sector 8 quests. Take the quest events early or treat them as pure combat encounters.
  • Hazards other than cloudOnly two things in this pool bring a hazard the cloud did not: the asteroid field at Auto-ship fight in asteroid field and the red giant at Boarders: Humans near sun — one a forced fight, one a forced boarding, and both readable one jump ahead with Long-Ranged Scanners.

"Unique" is the files' own unique="true". Whether that means once per sector or once per run is not stated in any file here; the wiki's better bet is once per sector.

The shipped event lists carry no weights, so no beacon here has stated odds. A pool entry listed twice is counted as ×2 and marked.
