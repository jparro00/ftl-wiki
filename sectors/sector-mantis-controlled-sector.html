Sector profile · MANTIS_SECTOR

Mantis Controlled Sector

Mantis space comes at you through the airlock, and it does not restock you afterwards. One to two guaranteed stores and — unlike Pirate space, which carries four — a single event in the whole pool that can open another. Six to seven hostile beacons plus one to two boarding parties, no nebula anywhere to slow the Rebels, and not one event here that buys you a jump of delay.

Solid blocks are the minimum the sector must place; faded blocks are the optional extra.

Lines are filled in this order, and the map holds at most 24 beacons (19–24 by the community wiki's count). When they run out, generation stops — a line near the bottom can receive nothing at all.

This sector allocates up to 26 slots against 24 beacons, so the bottom of the table is a wish list rather than a promise.

Beacons still empty at the end of the table are filled from the shared NEUTRAL list (OVERRIDE_NEUTRAL under Advanced Edition).

The exit beacon is not in this table at all — it draws from the shared EXIT_LIST, and an exit inside a cloud is always empty.

Entry beacon is always START_BEACON_MANTIS.

Versus the Mantis Homeworlds: identical, entry for entry — the same seven lists at the same minimums and the same maximums. The Homeworlds add exactly one thing, the guaranteed MANTIS_NAMED_THIEF beacon and the ship unlock behind it. This sector is that sector with the named quest cut out.

What can bite

  • BoardersBoarders: Mantis is a derelict that beams its survivors across — two to four Mantis, no decision offered. It is the only event listed twice in a two-event pool that must fill one to two beacons.
  • Boarders + sunBoarders: Humans near sun is the other half of that pool: two to four human pirates aboard, fought inside a solar flare, with fires starting behind you.
  • CrewFour events can kill a crew member. Escape pod does it fastest — one of three outcomes has the Mantis inside cut the nearest person in half and then fight on as a boarder.
  • TrapsBoth halves of Mantis fugitive can turn. Siding with the fugitive can cost 3 hull and two systems before the Engi ship even fires; selling him out can spring a Mantis on your deck instead.
  • DistressThe distress pool is where the crew dies: giant spiders, a station fire and a quarantine riot each have an outcome that takes someone off the roster.
  • AttritionNothing in this sector delays the Rebel fleet and no nebula beacon exists to halve its advance, so every jump you spend clearing boarders is a jump paid at full price.

Blue options that pay here

  • CloakingThe most useful thing to own in this sector. It skips the fight outright at Mantis fight choice, and grades the payout three ways at Malfunctioning defense system — level 1 is a poor deal, level 3 the best on offer.
  • No Mantis gateThe sector named after them contains not one blue option that asks for Mantis crew. The only species gates in the pool are Rockman and Engi.
  • Rockman / EngiBoth land on the station distress calls: a Rock walks into the fire at Fire on research station and is paid in an augment, and either a Rock or an Engi settles the riot at Unknown disease on mining colony with nobody exposed.
  • Anti-bio kitGiant alien spiders has three separate outs. The Anti-Bio Beam is the clean one — best payout, nothing spent; the Anti-Personnel and Boarding drones each burn a drone part.
  • Adv. FTL NavigationTurns Escort civilians FTL haywire into an upload: a high reward on the spot, and no quest beacon to fly to. Here that is a real trade rather than a free win — the marker you skip is the pool's only shot at an extra store.
  • Weapon typesAn ion weapon is the free win at Malfunctioning defense system; a beam or combat beam drone opens Crushed pirate; missiles open Asteroid mining colony.

What you can leave with

  • Nothing namedNo named augment, weapon or drone exists anywhere in this pool. Every payout is a generic reward tier, which makes the store the only place your loadout reliably changes — and there are one or two of those.
  • CrewSixteen events can end with someone joining, but only two name a species — Escape pod and Mantis fugitive both hand over a Mantis, and the escape pod can instead hold a human survivor of Mantis captivity.
  • CrewFire on research station pays a scientist if you dock and cut in, at a cost of 3 hull and a random system. Crew hiring station is the reliable version of the same idea.
  • RepairsMantis ship attacking civilian is the one place a saved civilian pays back in kind: 5 hull repaired, a free weapon installed, or a crew member, depending on who you rescued.
  • The best gated payoutsAn augment from the Rock at the fire, a weapon from Engi Med-bot Dispersal at the disease colony, a drone from a Repair drone at the fire. All three need the kit before you arrive.

Reading the map here

  • DistressThe distress-tagged events here are exactly the distress list, in both directions — nothing outside it carries the tag, nothing inside it is missing one. The markers you can count are the one to three the table allocated and no more, which is not true of most sectors.
  • ScannersThree of the eight arrive with a ship already parked at the beacon: Escort civilians FTL haywire and Friendly ship out of fuel, both friendly, and Pirate ship distress trap, a pirate lying in wait. A ship reading on a distress marker narrows it to those three, one of which opens fire.
  • StoresOne to two, placed before anything else, and then effectively nothing. Arriving here already needing hull and fuel is the mistake this sector punishes hardest.
  • No nebulaNo nebula line, so no purple clouds are drawn: sensors work everywhere, no beacon halves the Rebel advance, the exit is never a dead nebula stop, and the rule that pushes quest markers out of cloud cover never fires.
  • LeftoversThe table's minimum is well under the map's ceiling, so beacons can be left over and filled from the shared filler list — which is where the pirate briber and the fleet-delaying mercenary live. Neither appears in any of this sector's own lists.

Generation rules per the community's reverse-engineering of the map generator; the game files state none of them.

minSector in the files is zero-indexed, so the "earliest sector" chip is that value plus one — the number a player counts from the start of a run. The community wiki states the same offset.

The 24-beacon ceiling follows from the 6×4 grid; the 19-beacon floor is stated by the community wiki alone and is not derivable from anything else here, so the at-risk marks use the ceiling.

"Unique" is the files' own unique="true". Whether that means once per sector or once per run is not stated in any file here; the wiki's better bet is once per sector.

The shipped event lists carry no weights, so no beacon here has stated odds. A pool entry listed twice is counted as ×2 and marked.
