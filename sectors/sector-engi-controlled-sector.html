Sector profile · ENGI_SECTOR

Engi Controlled Sector

The Engi sector you actually meet — no depth requirement, no once-per-run limit, so a run can fly through two of them. It is the best-supplied sector in the game: as many guaranteed stores as anywhere, more item beacons than any other sector, and a hostile list with no Engi ship in it. The catch is arithmetic. Even the sector's minimum ask is as large as the smallest map the game builds, so everything below the store line is competing for whatever the map happens to have.

The engine builds its candidate list from every species whose rarity here is not 0, weights each one 6 − rarity, and fills each of the three crew slots with its own independent draw — so a store can offer the same species twice. "Per slot" is that species' share of the total weight; "in a store" is the chance it turns up in at least one of the three slots.

Weighting read out of FTLGame_orig.exe, not from any data file: the candidate loop skips rarity 0 and computes 6 − rarity as the weight. The three-slot count is the Advanced Edition figure, which is the data these pages are built from; vanilla rolls two or three.

Left of the arrow is the blueprint's base rarity, right of it is this sector's. 1 is the commonest and 5 the rarest; 0 is a flag rather than a low number — that entry is not offered here at all. A sector that declares no rarity list of its own uses the base values throughout.

Per the community wiki, the number does two jobs: it weights what a store offers, and it gates event rewards without weighting them — nonzero here means a crew-kill reward can hand you that species, 0 means it never can. The game files state neither. No file anywhere on this machine gives the weighting function, so nothing on this page turns a rarity into odds.

Solid blocks are the minimum the sector must place; faded blocks are the optional extra. Open a line to see every event it can place; open an event to read its card here.

Lines are filled in this order, and the map holds at most 24 beacons (19–24 by the community wiki's count). When they run out, generation stops — a line near the bottom can receive nothing at all.

This sector allocates up to 27 slots against 24 beacons, so the bottom of the table is a wish list rather than a promise.

Beacons still empty at the end of the table are filled from the shared NEUTRAL list (OVERRIDE_NEUTRAL under Advanced Edition). That line is not in the file — the engine reaches the list by name — so it is marked rather than numbered in the table above.

Here that is up to 5 beacons: a full 24-beacon map, less the 19 this table must place.

The exit beacon is not in this table at all — it draws from the shared EXIT_LIST, and an exit inside a cloud is always empty.

Entry beacon is always START_BEACON_ENGI.

Versus Engi Homeworlds: the same eight lists in the same order, two differences — Homeworlds bolts a named ENGI_UNLOCK_1 beacon onto the front, where nothing can crowd it out, and gives NEUTRAL_ENGI 5–7 instead of 4–6. That makes its table two slots hungrier against the same ceiling, so the squeeze at the bottom is worse there and the Stealth Cruiser start is still safe. Here nothing is placed by name at all: no ship unlock, no set-piece, every beacon drawn from a pool.

Blue options that pay here

  • Engi ×4The virus, the smashed ships, the Mantis-held station and the defence satellite. It is the only way to get the base marker out of Engi ship attacked by Mantis ship, and on The Engi virus it is the branch that gets a crewman killed.
  • Hacking ×2Three graded outs inside The Engi virus — quarantine it, or reprogram it for 15 hull and a reactor bar, or 30 hull at level 3, all three ending with the augment. Also the only alternative to crippled engines in Pirate engine hacker.
  • Sensors ×7The widest gate in the sector. Level 2 tells you what is aboard the burning station in Engi research station before you commit, which turns its coin-flip into a clean crew pickup; level 2 or 3 also opens Battlefield wreckage, Deactivated Auto-ship and Engi ship attacked by Mantis ship.
  • Long-Ranged Scanners ×4Earns twice over: it reads the distress beacons before you spend the jump, and it opens all three events that can plant the hidden-base marker — Encrypted federation signal, Asteroid belt distress and Engi ship attacked by Mantis ship — plus Engi research station.
  • Cloaking ×3Ways out of Mantis fight choice, the storage station in Auto-ship near storage station and the satellite in Malfunctioning defense system, which grades its payoff by cloak level.
  • Lanius ×1One clean solve on The Engi virus — scrap and the augment, no fight and nobody lost.

What you can leave with

  • AugmentDamaged Stasis Pod from Dense asteroid field distress — the Crystal route starts here, and there is no fight anywhere under it. Searching plainly costs 4 hull and an engine hit; Rock Plating searches for free.
  • CrewA Crystal crew member at Zoltan research facility, if you arrive already carrying the pod.
  • Augment · WeaponEngi Med-bot Dispersal from Engi distress Rebel fight for 40 scrap, 2 missiles and 2 fuel; the Healing Burst bomb is one of three draws off the cheaper 25-scrap option.
  • AugmentDrone Reactor Booster from The Engi virus — every Hacking and Lanius branch hands it over.
  • CrewAn Engi with two levels in every skill: take the Engi option on The Engi virus, lose the crewman to the virus, win the fight, and it walks back out.
  • ScrapFree scrap with resources (Engi) and Engi surrender are free standard rewards with nothing asked in return — two of the six events on the three Engi item beacons.

What can bite

  • CrewFive events can cost you a crew member: Giant alien spiders, Engi research station, Confused Mantis, Merchant's request, and the Engi branch of The Engi virus.
  • BoardersSix events put someone aboard — up to four at Refueling platform, three to four at Merchant's request, two at Zoltan research facility, Mantis from Engi ship attacked by Mantis ship and Mantis fugitive, two to three humans on one draw of Encrypted federation signal.
  • SystemsThe Engi virus halves your shields and engines before the fight if you try to purge it yourself, and Pirate engine hacker holds engines at one bar until the pirate is dead.
  • The fleetRebel ship warning is a fight with no choice attached, and letting the scout jump away costs a jump of your lead. Pirate briber can buy one back, but only on the branch where you fight the pirate and the victim turns out to be a Rebel scout.

Reading the map here

  • No nebulaThis sector allocates no nebula line, so no purple cloud is ever drawn on it. Sensors work at every beacon, nothing on the map halves the fleet's advance for you, and the exit always draws a real event instead of the empty one a cloud would force.
  • Quest markersA marker cannot be dropped on a store, the exit, a nebula beacon, a distress beacon, a beacon you have already visited, one the fleet has taken, or the one you are sitting on — and it has to be closer to you than the Rebels are to it. When nothing qualifies, the quest is pushed into the next sector instead.
  • Markers appear lateDistress and store beacons only light up once you are one jump from them, so the map fills in as you go rather than up front. A distress marker then stays put until the fleet reaches it — there is no timer on it beyond the one chasing you.
  • Two of these in a runNothing stops the sector repeating, and a second Engi sector re-rolls the whole table from scratch — another two to three stores, another five item beacons, another shot at the stasis pod.

Generation rules per the community's reverse-engineering of the map generator; the game files state none of them.

minSector in the files is zero-indexed, so the "earliest sector" chip is that value plus one — the number a player counts from the start of a run. The community wiki states the same offset.

The 24-beacon ceiling follows from the 6×4 grid; the 19-beacon floor is stated by the community wiki alone and is not derivable from anything else here, so the at-risk marks use the ceiling.

"Unique" is the files' own unique="true". Whether that means once per sector or once per run is not stated in any file here; the wiki's better bet is once per sector.

The shipped event lists carry no weights, so no beacon here has stated odds. A pool entry listed twice is counted as ×2 and marked.
