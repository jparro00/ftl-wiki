Sector profile · NEBULA_SECTOR

Uncharted Nebula

Almost everything this sector is required to place sits inside the cloud, and the events are written around the blackout: your Sensors do nothing, and you cannot tell whether the intruders are already aboard, what is inside the derelict station, or where the damaged Federation ship went. The fog is supposed to buy time — but the Rebels came into this sector prepared for it, so a nebula jump here shortens their advance by only about a fifth where the same beacon elsewhere would halve it, and four events in the pool hand them a whole extra jump while nothing gives one back.

Solid blocks are the minimum the sector must place; faded blocks are the optional extra. Open a line to see every event it can place; open an event to see its card.

Lines are filled in this order, and the map holds at most 24 beacons (19–24 by the community wiki's count). When they run out, generation stops — a line near the bottom can receive nothing at all.

This sector allocates up to 26 slots against 24 beacons, so the bottom of the table is a wish list rather than a promise.

Placed before everything else, whatever the file order, because the cloud graphics come first: NEBULA_STORE, NEBULA_EMPTY, NEBULA_HOSTILE, NEBULA_NEUTRAL. A cloud drawn over an ordinary beacon converts it, and that beacon draws from the shared NEBULA list instead.

Beacons still empty at the end of the table are filled from the shared NEUTRAL list (OVERRIDE_NEUTRAL under Advanced Edition).

The exit beacon is not in this table at all — it draws from the shared EXIT_LIST, and an exit inside a cloud is always empty.

Entry beacon is always START_BEACON_NEBULA.

Versus the Slug nebulas: they ask for up to 34 slots on a map that holds 24, so their bottom lines routinely get nothing. This sector asks for 26, which means nearly everything it lists actually reaches a beacon — and the only line with real slack is the last one, the distress calls. What you give up for that tidiness is a shop: the Slugs guarantee two nebula stores, this sector guarantees one.

Blue options that pay here

  • Cloaking ×4The sector's best key: past the guard at Auto-ship near storage station in nebula, out of the storm fight at Auto-ship fight in plasma storm, past the Rebel picket at Rebel fight choice in nebula, and graded across three levels at Malfunctioning defense system.
  • Long-Ranged Scanners ×4The blackout workaround — tracks the drifting crew at Nebula lost ship and holds the lock at Rebel fight chance in nebula, which otherwise loses them or loses you.
  • Lifeform Scanner · Sensors 3Both buy the same certain engagement at Rebel fight chance in nebula — but only the augment still works once you are in the cloud, and Sensors' other payoff, Trade scrap for upgrades, is one of the few beacons here that is not in one.
  • Engines ×2Level 3 or 6 to run from Auto-ship fight in plasma storm, level 4 to break contact at Rebel fight choice in nebula once you have been spotted.
  • Crew Teleporter ×3The clean answer at Nebula lost ship — a crew member and scrap with no roll at all — plus options at Asteroid belt distress and Rebel ship attacking Federation loyalists.
  • Hacking · RockmanHacking opens two graded routes into Auto-ship near storage station in nebula, both costing a drone part; Rockman pays at Rock ship in plasma storm, Fire on research station and Unknown disease on mining colony.

What you can leave with

  • CrewOver half the pool can hand you someone — Nebula lost ship, Plasma storm incapacitated ships, Single life form on moon and the hiring station at Crew hiring station among them.
  • WeaponsNo named item drops in this sector — every weapon in the pool is a random one. Pirate ship selling weapon sells one unseen for 45 scrap, and one branch takes the scrap and hands over nothing.
  • The stationAuto-ship near storage station in nebula is the sector's loot box — a drone schematic, a weapon, a haul of resources, or a stripped hull with nothing in it.
  • FuelTrade resources in nebula swaps fuel, missiles and drone parts in both directions, Pirate smuggler pays a fuel bribe to a Weapon Control 6 threat, and Refueling station sits in the item pool.

What can bite

  • Decisions made blindYou cannot confirm you have been boarded at Boarders: Humans in nebula, cannot see inside the hull at Auto-ship near storage station in nebula, and cannot find the Federation ship at Nebula lost ship without help. The writing assumes the blackout.
  • Plasma storms ×4Rebel fight in plasma storm, Auto-ship fight in plasma storm, Plasma storm incapacitated ships and Rock ship in plasma storm trade fog for a storm and half a reactor; Rebel fight in plasma storm starts shooting on arrival. Zoltan crew and a Backup Battery are the only power the storm does not touch.
  • Boarders ×3Boarders: Humans in nebula (2–4 humans) and Boarders: rebels in nebula (3–4) open with an alarm and no ship to shoot back at. Refugee comms down is the third.
  • Crew loss ×6Six events can cost a crew member outright. Plasma storm incapacitated ships is the sector's own: searching the wrecks blind also risks 4 hull damage and a breach.

Reading the map here

  • A clear beacon is not a promiseThe clouds are laid down before a single event is allocated, and one drawn across an ordinary beacon takes it over. So you can jump to a beacon that shows no fog and arrive in one anyway, running an event from a shared list that appears in no pool below.
  • Sensors are dead weightInside a cloud the system does nothing — no enemy interiors, no charge bars, whatever level you bought. Slug crew, which these stores carry more readily than any other non-human, and the Lifeform Scanner are the only two things that still see.
  • The fleet clockA jump from a nebula beacon here cuts the fleet's advance by about a fifth; the same jump in an ordinary sector cuts it by half. That thin discount is the whole payoff for flying blind — and Auto-ship warning in nebula, Rebel fight choice in nebula, Rebel fight chance in nebula and Asteroid belt distress each hand back a full extra jump, with nothing in the pool to offset them.
  • If they catch youA nebula beacon never carries an Anti-Ship Battery, so an overtaken beacon here is an Elite fighter and nothing else — unless you are sitting on it out of fuel, which strips the cloud and brings the battery. Every nebula beacon the fleet takes gets an ion storm instead, so you fight on half a reactor; the exit beacon is the exception.
  • ShopsOne store is guaranteed and placed before anything can crowd it out, with a second only sometimes — and roughly 0.8% of these sectors generate with none at all. Escort civilians FTL haywire, in the distress pool, can open one more.
  • Quest markersA marker will only land on a beacon that is not a nebula, not a store, not a distress call, not the exit and not already taken by the fleet — and this sector is almost all cloud. Start a quest at Rebel ship attacking Federation loyalists or Asteroid belt distress and there is often nowhere legal to put it, so it follows you into the next sector instead.

Generation rules per the community's reverse-engineering of the map generator; the game files state none of them.

minSector in the files is zero-indexed, so the "earliest sector" chip is that value plus one — the number a player counts from the start of a run. The community wiki states the same offset.

The 24-beacon ceiling follows from the 6×4 grid; the 19-beacon floor is stated by the community wiki alone and is not derivable from anything else here, so the at-risk marks use the ceiling.

"Unique" is the files' own unique="true". Whether that means once per sector or once per run is not stated in any file here; the wiki's better bet is once per sector.

The shipped event lists carry no weights, so no beacon here has stated odds. A pool entry listed twice is counted as ×2 and marked.
