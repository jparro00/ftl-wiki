Sector profile · REBEL_SECTOR

Rebel Controlled Sector

Rebel space, held by the fleet that is already chasing you — the sector where the pursuit stops being background weather and becomes something your choices move. Seven of the 49 events here touch the fleet clock, and six of them can only push it the wrong way: a scout that lives long enough to charge its FTL doubles that jump's advance. The counterweight is not an event at all — reaching a nebula beacon halves the advance, so the sector's most dependable brake is whatever cloud the map happens to draw.

Solid blocks are the minimum the sector must place; faded blocks are the optional extra. Open a line to see every event it can place; open an event to read its card here.

Lines are filled in this order, and the map holds at most 24 beacons (19–24 by the community wiki's count). When they run out, generation stops — a line near the bottom can receive nothing at all.

This sector allocates up to 30 slots against 24 beacons, so the bottom of the table is a wish list rather than a promise.

Could be cut entirely if everything above rolls high: NEUTRAL_REBEL.

Placed before everything else, whatever the file order, because the cloud graphics come first: NEBULA_REBEL. A cloud drawn over an ordinary beacon converts it, and that beacon draws from the shared NEBULA list instead.

Beacons still empty at the end of the table are filled from the shared NEUTRAL list (OVERRIDE_NEUTRAL under Advanced Edition).

The exit beacon is not in this table at all — it draws from the shared EXIT_LIST, and an exit inside a cloud is always empty.

Entry beacon is always START_BEACON_REBEL.

Two sectors hide in this one table, and they are mutually exclusive. Roll high everywhere and the map fills before the last line is reached — the neutral pool goes, and with it Auto-ship near radar station, the only event in the sector that can push the fleet back. Roll low and the table runs out before the beacons do, and the leftovers are drawn from a shared list that is not Rebel-flavoured at all — the one way a mercenary selling two jumps of delay ever appears here.

Blue options that pay here

  • Cloaking ×4The default way out: past the storage-cache guard in clear space and in nebula, out of the plasma storm, and clean past the Rebel in Rebel fight choice in nebula. Only Auto-ship near storage station rolls for detection no matter what level you are carrying.
  • Crew Teleporter ×5Boards the sensor station without provoking its guard, pulls the lost Federation crew out of the nebula, and is one of four ways to save the loyalists at Rebel ship attacking Federation loyalists — the one that gets you a crew member and the base coordinates.
  • Hacking ×2Auto-ship near radar station is the only brake on the pursuit in the whole pool: the Hacking branch delays the fleet a jump and reveals the map for one drone part, where the plain approach is a four-way roll that can trip an alarm and cost you a jump instead.
  • Engines ×2Pure escape, and graded — level 3 is a coin flip out of the plasma storm, level 6 is certain, level 4 loses the Rebel inside the cloud at Rebel fight choice in nebula.
  • Sensors ×6The widest gate in the sector once the quest stages are counted — and dead weight at every nebula beacon, where the cloud shuts sensors off entirely. Level 3 reads the sensor station's map from a distance and scans Deactivated Auto-ship instead of waking it; level 2 and Long-Ranged Scanners both salvage the Federation base's dead end.
  • Rock crew / Repair droneFire on research station kills a crew member on anyone who walks in unaided. A Rockman walks through the fire for a high augment reward; a System Repair Drone does the same job for a drone schematic.

What you can leave with

  • Crew ×23Twenty-three of the forty-nine events can hand you someone — by a distance the most common thing this sector gives, and mostly prisoners and survivors pulled out of ships you have just beaten.
  • Weapons & dronesRebel transport ship is the richest single roll in the pool — eleven destroyed-outcomes covering military weaponry, a drone schematic, drone parts, a map reveal and a prisoner.
  • Augments ×2Only Fire on research station behind Rock crew and Asteroid mining colony for fifteen missiles produce one, and neither names it — both roll a random augment.
  • HullThe hidden Federation base's space-dock outcome repairs 35, the largest patch anything in reach of this sector offers. Short of that it is Repair station at 5, 10 or 20 for scrap and Asteroid mining colony at 5 to 15 for missiles.
  • A RebelRebel defector is the only event in the pool that names the species it gives you — a human, three times out of six, sometimes with a scrap cache attached.

What can bite

  • The pursuit ×6Auto-ship warning, Rebel ship warning and Auto-ship warning in nebula if the scout escapes; Rebel fight choice in nebula if it spots you and lives; Rebel defector if the defector was lying; Asteroid belt distress if you leave the civilians to die and they survive to report you.
  • Boarders ×6One beacon of them is guaranteed, but six events can put people aboard: up to four humans at Boarders: Humans near sun, Boarders: rebels in nebula and the infected station on Merchant's request, two to three at Rebel fight with boarders and Encrypted federation signal, one or two at Rebel defector.
  • Crew loss ×4Plasma storm incapacitated ships, Fire on research station, Rebel defector and Merchant's request are the only events that can take someone off the roster, and every one of them is a branch you opt into — nothing here costs crew on arrival.
  • Drone partsAuto-ship near radar station charges a drone part for the approach whether or not it works, including the outcome where the drone is shot down and the auto-ship comes for you anyway.
  • BaitPirate ship distress trap is a distress call with a pirate behind it, and one of the five outcomes at Encrypted federation signal is a Rebel signal broadcast specifically to catch ships like yours.

Reading the map here

  • The clouds are the brakeReaching a nebula beacon halves that jump's advance, and how many the map has is settled before a single event is assigned. A cloud-free roll leaves the radar station as the only thing in the sector that can slow the fleet at all — and that sits in the line most likely to be cut.
  • If it catches youA Rebel Elite Fighter under an anti-ship battery: three hull and a guaranteed breach per real shot, one fuel for the kill and no scrap, because the armada stops you stripping the wreck. Inside a cloud there is no battery — the beacon gains an ion storm instead, half your reactor and no breaches.
  • StoresOne or two are guaranteed and the line is filled early enough that the squeeze never reaches it. Escort civilians FTL haywire can open a third, and the shared fallback list carries a pirate briber who will sell you into one.
  • Quest markersA planted marker still needs somewhere legal to land: an unvisited beacon, outside the clouds, not already taken by the fleet, and nearer to you than the fleet is to it. In the sector where the fleet is the whole problem, that filter is what quietly pushes quests into the next one.

Generation rules per the community's reverse-engineering of the map generator; the game files state none of them.

minSector in the files is zero-indexed, so the "earliest sector" chip is that value plus one — the number a player counts from the start of a run. The community wiki states the same offset.

The 24-beacon ceiling follows from the 6×4 grid; the 19-beacon floor is stated by the community wiki alone and is not derivable from anything else here, so the at-risk marks use the ceiling.

"Unique" is the files' own unique="true". Whether that means once per sector or once per run is not stated in any file here; the wiki's better bet is once per sector.

The shipped event lists carry no weights, so no beacon here has stated odds. A pool entry listed twice is counted as ×2 and marked.

Defined in the files both as an event list and as a single event, and read here as the list: NEBULA_REBEL.
