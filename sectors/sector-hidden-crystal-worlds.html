Hidden Crystal Worlds

The sector you cannot fly to. Nothing on the map routes here — a Crystal crewmember brings you, and the ship-unlock marker is planted on the arrival beacon before you have made a single choice. It is also the only sector in the game whose allocation cannot fit: its minimum demand is one slot larger than the biggest map the game can build, so it always leaves something unplaced, and always the same something. Twenty-four events are all it has to fill that map with.

Solid blocks are the minimum the sector must place; faded blocks are the optional extra.

Lines are filled in this order, and the map holds at most 24 beacons (19–24 by the community wiki's count). When they run out, generation stops — a line near the bottom can receive nothing at all.

This sector allocates up to 31 slots against 24 beacons, so the bottom of the table is a wish list rather than a promise.

This sector's minimum allocation is 25 against 24 beacons, so it can never place everything it asks for.

Cannot be filled even in the best case — the minima above it already use up the map: NEUTRAL_CRYSTAL.

Beacons still empty at the end of the table are filled from the shared NEUTRAL list (OVERRIDE_NEUTRAL under Advanced Edition).

The exit beacon is not in this table at all — it draws from the shared EXIT_LIST, and an exit inside a cloud is always empty.

Entry beacon is always START_BEACON_CRYSTAL.

Everything above the neutral line gets placed in full. The five lines ahead of it come to at most nineteen, so the stores, the item beacons, the empties, the fights and the boarders are never what gets cut — the whole shortfall lands on the bottom line, every run. Two things follow. The shared filler list that tops up leftover beacons everywhere else can never be reached here, because this table runs out of map before it runs out of lines. And with a pool this small, repetition is arithmetic rather than bad luck: twelve neutral slots draw on ten events of which six are once-only, and six to ten hostile slots draw on five of which three are.

Blue options that pay here

  • Crystal ×2Your own Crystalline Being retunes the ship's weapons and opens Crystalline cache with no roll at all, and turns Crystal chat into free supplies.
  • Breach Missiles ×1The other clean way into the cache — one missile spent, instead of firing everything you have and mostly bouncing off.
  • Engines 7 · Teleporter 2Both pull your crewmember back out of the cache's singularity trap. Engines keep the cargo as well; the teleporter saves the person only.
  • Rockman · Backup DNA BankTwo ways through Crystalline research facility without putting a body on the table — the Rockman is an evolutionary cousin, the DNA bank is data they have never seen.
  • Distraction Buoys ×1Worth carrying in twice over: it postpones the fleet a turn as you arrive, and at Crystalline ship messaging about Rebels it takes the scrap and the jump of delay with none of the chance of being caught faking it.
  • Four events, seven gatesOnly four events in the whole pool gate anything, and Crystalline cache carries four of the seven gates by itself.

Named things you can leave with

  • WeaponHeavy Crystal Mark II from Crystalline men buried — but only by sending a crewmember down to the dig and then waiting out two full cycles.
  • WeaponCrystal Lockdown Bomb or Crystal Burst Mark II from Crystal scrap collector: pay the collector 35 scrap, then ask for payment in kind rather than taking him aboard.
  • CrewA Crystal crewmember two ways — that same collector, or accepting the surrender at Crystal fight and agreeing to take one of the survivors.
  • CrewA Human, out of the cargo hold of the ship that was hunting them, if Crystal fight with surrender offer (Human crew) surrenders.
  • WeaponA Crystal weapon off the cache at Crystalline cache, and off rescuing the ship under fire at Rebel ship attacking Crystal ship, Mantis ship attacking Crystal or Pirate ship attacking Crystal.
  • HullCrystal fight with surrender offer (hull repairs) repairs 8 if you stop the fight once the escort falters; Crystal chat repairs 6 on one of the three ways the conversation can go.

What can bite

  • CrewThree events can cost a crewmember and all three are your own choice: the singularity in Crystalline cache, the dig at Crystalline men buried, and letting the scientists at Crystalline research facility run their tests.
  • BoardersThree events put Crystalline crew on your ship — two to three at Boarders: Crystal and again if the cache's owners are home, one or two at Crystalline ship messaging about Rebels if they catch you lying.
  • FleetCrystalline men buried advances the Rebel fleet a jump for every cycle you wait, and the Heavy Crystal is behind two of them. Rebel ship attacking Crystal ship advances it one if you shoot the Crystal ship rather than the Rebel.
  • FuelThe cache escape that saves both the crewmember and the cargo detonates your entire fuel reserve to do it. Advanced Engines do the same job for nothing.
  • RefusalsCrystalline research facility can answer a flat no by undocking a warship that will not accept surrender. Crystalline men buried is safe to decline outright — it only turns hostile if you promise help and then pull your crewmember back out.

Reading the map here

  • Nothing is markedNot one event in this pool carries a distress tag, so no beacon here is ever labelled as one. The stores, the exit and the quest are the only things the map will ever name; every other beacon is opaque until you are standing on it.
  • ScannersNo event in the sector sets an environment — no asteroid field, no sun, no pulsar anywhere in the pool — so Long-Ranged Scanners have nothing to warn you about. Here the augment is a ship detector and nothing else.
  • The quest markerPlanted on arrival, before your first jump, and it lands on a beacon that already had an event — which it overwrites. The cruiser costs you one of the sector's encounters as well as the trip out to it.
  • The fleetThe Rebels follow you through here like anywhere else, and there is no nebula in this sector to duck into, so the one universal way of halving their advance is off the table. Exactly one branch in the whole pool pushes them back; everything else that touches them pushes the wrong way.
  • HazardsThe only environmental danger you will meet is the one the Rebels bring: a beacon they have taken overwrites whatever was on it, often with an anti-ship battery attached.
  • The exitNot in the table below — it draws from a list every sector shares, and with no cloud cover in this sector it never falls back to the empty nebula exit. It is also the last beacon that can still turn into a shop.

Generation rules per the community's reverse-engineering of the map generator; the game files state none of them.

minSector in the files is zero-indexed, so the "earliest sector" chip is that value plus one — the number a player counts from the start of a run. The community wiki states the same offset.

The 24-beacon ceiling follows from the 6×4 grid; the 19-beacon floor is stated by the community wiki alone and is not derivable from anything else here, so the at-risk marks use the ceiling.

"Unique" is the files' own unique="true". Whether that means once per sector or once per run is not stated in any file here; the wiki's better bet is once per sector.

The shipped event lists carry no weights, so no beacon here has stated odds. A pool entry listed twice is counted as ×2 and marked.
