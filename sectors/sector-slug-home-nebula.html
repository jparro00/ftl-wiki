Sector profile · SLUG_HOME

Slug Home Nebula

Purple usually means a breather. Here a cloud beacon shaves only about a fifth off the Rebel advance instead of halving it, your sensors are dead across most of the map, and the Slugs open a fight by switching one of your systems off before they shoot. The table asks for more than the map can hold, and what goes hungry is the clear-space half of it — the straight fights, the distress calls, the storms — while the clouds and the one guaranteed unlock beacon are placed regardless.

Solid blocks are the minimum the sector must place; faded blocks are the optional extra. Open a line to see every event it can place; open an event to see its card.

Lines are filled in this order, and the map holds at most 24 beacons (19–24 by the community wiki's count). When they run out, generation stops — a line near the bottom can receive nothing at all.

This sector allocates up to 35 slots against 24 beacons, so the bottom of the table is a wish list rather than a promise.

Could be cut entirely if everything above rolls high: HOSTILE_SLUG, DISTRESS_BEACON_SLUG, STORM_SLUG, NEUTRAL.

Placed before everything else, whatever the file order, because the cloud graphics come first: NEBULA_SLUG_FIGHT_UNLOCK, NEBULA_STORE_SLUG, NEBULA_NOTHING_SLUG, NEBULA_HOSTILE_SLUG, NEBULA_NEUTRAL_SLUG. A cloud drawn over an ordinary beacon converts it, and that beacon draws from the shared NEBULA list instead.

Beacons still empty at the end of the table are filled from the shared NEUTRAL list (OVERRIDE_NEUTRAL under Advanced Edition).

The exit beacon is not in this table at all — it draws from the shared EXIT_LIST, and an exit inside a cloud is always empty.

Entry beacon is always START_BEACON_SLUG.

Versus a plain Slug Controlled Nebula: the two definitions are identical apart from the unlock beacon — same lists, same minimums, same maximums, same rarity table, same start beacon. That one extra beacon is spent before the rest of the table gets a look in, so the sibling sector is this one with a beacon more of slack at the bottom: whatever gets squeezed out here is squeezed a little later there. Both are gated to sector 4 at the earliest — the file's minSector="3" counts from zero — and only this one is once per run.

Blue options that pay here

  • Hacking ×6The single best system to fly in with. It cancels the remote hack in Slug hacker (doors), Slug hacker (oxygen), Slug hacker (medical) and Slug hacker (choice) — four fights that otherwise open with a system already at zero — and sweetens Auto-ship near storage station in nebula and Pirate ship selling drones on top.
  • Slug ×6The crewman who cancels the sector's own blackout, and the game says so in his description. A telepathic duel that can end The Black Raven without a shot, the ambush spotted early in Slug store ship, the dying survivor located in Nebula wreckage, an Engi out of Intelligent ponies with no dice roll attached — and the quiet route through the unlock chain.
  • Sensors ×7Level 2 or 3 opens a branch in six events, Rebel fight chance in nebula, Deactivated Auto-ship, Battlefield wreckage and Terraforming scan among them — and level 2 is the non-Slug way through the unlock chain, where the text has you overclocking the system to punch through the clouds.
  • Medbay 2 ×3Keeps the system half-alive under the hack in Slug hacker (medical), saves the dying survivor in Nebula wreckage for a crew member, and cures the infected crewman in Plagued station.
  • Cloaking ×3Clean exits from Rebel fight choice in nebula, Auto-ship near storage station in nebula and Auto-ship near storage station. Hiding from that Rebel patrol without it can instead lose you a jump to the fleet.
  • Mantis / RockmanOne crew member each turns a Slug trap around: the Mantis walks into Slug oxygen malfunction expecting it, and the Rockman drinks whatever is in the flask at Slug drink on your behalf.

Named things you can leave with

  • WeaponAnti-Bio Beam — the surrender offer at Slug Home Nebula surrender. It is the same choice as the unlock chain, so you get one or the other.
  • CrewA named Slug crew member from Slocknog — he asks 55 scrap, and sometimes waives it if you start powering up the FTL instead.
  • CrewA Slug at Slug moons question: the arrival text tells you how many moons the planet has, and that is the answer. Guessing costs scrap, fuel and drone parts.
  • CrewAn Engi at Intelligent ponies, a human at Plagued station, and a Mantis, Rockman or Engi at Slaver (friendly).
  • WeaponAn unnamed weapon for 45 scrap from Pirate ship selling weapon, who may also take the scrap and hand over nothing. Mind Control gets him talking about what it actually is.

What can bite

  • SystemsThe hacker family is the real hazard: doors locked open next to fire weapons, oxygen cut, medbay dark with boarders already aboard, or shields, oxygen or weapons halved by your own answer at Slug hacker (choice).
  • BoardersSix events put someone on your ship. Slug hacker (medical) lands two Slugs while your medbay is off, and Boarders: Humans in plasma storm drops three to four humans on you with no ship fight attached.
  • ScrapSlug repair station patches your hull and then clamps you to the station: 15 fuel or 50 scrap. Refusing means a planted bomb, damage and a fight anyway.
  • CrewOnly three events in the pool can kill a crew member — Slug oxygen malfunction, Intelligent ponies and Plagued station. This sector takes systems and scrap far more often than it takes people.
  • FuelThe empty entries can take up a lot of the map between them, and Empty nebula beacon (Slug) is the larger of the two. Every one of those is a jump spent for nothing, in a sector with two stores you can rely on.

Reading the map here

  • StoresTwo of the three are the pair inside the clouds, and they are rolled before the table can run dry — the only stores here you can plan a route around. Of the counters that open out of an event instead, Slug store ship is an ambush wearing a shopfront and Pirate briber is a toll booth that happens to sell things.
  • DistressEvery distress call this sector can advertise comes from the one line at the bottom that the map is most likely to starve — nothing else in the pool leaks a distress-tagged event in to pad the count. Three of that line's own events carry no tag either, so a quiet map is not evidence of a quiet sector.
  • Flying blindSensors do nothing at a cloud beacon, and most of this map is cloud: no enemy rooms, no enemy crew, no weapon charge bars. A Slug in the crew or a Lifeform Scanner is what puts the enemy back on your screen — both are written specifically to work where sensors do not.
  • Quest markersA marker can never be planted on a cloud beacon, a store, a distress beacon or the exit, so this sector has far fewer legal spots for one than it has beacons. A quest that finds nowhere to land waits for the next sector instead, and from sector 7 there is no next sector willing to take it.
  • The fleet behind youA cloud beacon the Rebels take always turns into an ion storm, so backtracking behind the front costs half your reactor as well as the jump. The compensation is that cloud beacons never carry anti-ship batteries, unless you are sitting at one out of fuel.

Generation rules per the community's reverse-engineering of the map generator; the game files state none of them.

minSector in the files is zero-indexed, so the "earliest sector" chip is that value plus one — the number a player counts from the start of a run. The community wiki states the same offset.

The 24-beacon ceiling follows from the 6×4 grid; the 19-beacon floor is stated by the community wiki alone and is not derivable from anything else here, so the at-risk marks use the ceiling.

"Unique" is the files' own unique="true". Whether that means once per sector or once per run is not stated in any file here; the wiki's better bet is once per sector.

The shipped event lists carry no weights, so no beacon here has stated odds. A pool entry listed twice is counted as ×2 and marked.
