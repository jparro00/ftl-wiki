Sector profile · ROCK_SECTOR

Rock Controlled Sector

Nothing here is placed by name — no unlock beacon, no wormhole — so what you get is decided entirely by the order the lines are filled. The stores and the distress beacons land before a single fight is placed and are effectively certain; the deep neutral pool at the bottom, the one carrying the Damaged Stasis Pod, is where a full map runs out of room. Rock space is generous at the top of the table and thin at the bottom.

The engine builds its candidate list from every species whose rarity here is not 0, weights each one 6 − rarity, and fills each of the three crew slots with its own independent draw — so a store can offer the same species twice. "Per slot" is that species' share of the total weight; "in a store" is the chance it turns up in at least one of the three slots.

Weighting read out of FTLGame_orig.exe, not from any data file: the candidate loop skips rarity 0 and computes 6 − rarity as the weight. The three-slot count is the Advanced Edition figure, which is the data these pages are built from; vanilla rolls two or three.

Left of the arrow is the blueprint's base rarity, right of it is this sector's. 1 is the commonest and 5 the rarest; 0 is a flag rather than a low number — that entry is not offered here at all. A sector that declares no rarity list of its own uses the base values throughout.

Per the community wiki, the number does two jobs: it weights what a store offers, and it gates event rewards without weighting them — nonzero here means a crew-kill reward can hand you that species, 0 means it never can. The game files state neither. No file anywhere on this machine gives the weighting function, so nothing on this page turns a rarity into odds.

Solid blocks are the minimum the sector must place; faded blocks are the optional extra. Open a line to see every event it can place; open an event to read its card here.

Lines are filled in this order, and the map holds at most 24 beacons (19–24 by the community wiki's count). When they run out, generation stops — a line near the bottom can receive nothing at all.

This sector allocates up to 28 slots against 24 beacons, so the bottom of the table is a wish list rather than a promise.

Beacons still empty at the end of the table are filled from the shared NEUTRAL list (OVERRIDE_NEUTRAL under Advanced Edition). That line is not in the file — the engine reaches the list by name — so it is marked rather than numbered in the table above.

Here that is up to 4 beacons: a full 24-beacon map, less the 20 this table must place.

The exit beacon is not in this table at all — it draws from the shared EXIT_LIST, and an exit inside a cloud is always empty.

Entry beacon is always START_BEACON_ROCK.

Versus the Rock Homeworlds: the same eight entries in the same order, the same pools, the same numbers. The homeworlds bolt two named beacons on top — the Rock Cruiser test and the Crystal wormhole — and because those sit above everything else in the definition, they are the two things that sector can never fail to place. This one repeats and names nothing; the homeworlds arrive at most once a run and no earlier than sector 5.

Blue options that pay here

  • Sensors ×5The widest gate in the sector. Level 2 turns the plain appeal at Rock atheists into a certain Rock crew member, and opens branches at Asteroid belt distress, Rebel ship attacking Federation loyalists, Encrypted federation signal and Trade scrap for upgrades.
  • Rockman ×3Mantis ship with Rock body parts, Fire on research station and Unknown disease on mining colony. A Rockman walks straight into the burning station and the scientists pay an augment for it; on the mining colony the immunity alone is worth a reward. Rockmen are also the crew most likely to be on the shelf in this sector's stores.
  • Rock Plating ×3Ram the hunter at Mantis ship with Rock body parts and its engines are dead before the shooting starts; guarantee the pod at Dense asteroid field distress; and a third opening at Asteroid belt distress.
  • Crew Teleporter ×4Slaver (friendly), Asteroid belt distress, Rebel ship attacking Federation loyalists and Capture the ship, where it is one of only three keys in, alongside an Anti-Bio Beam and a Fire Bomb.
  • System Repair Drone ×3Mantis ships battle for Rock freighter, Asteroid belt distress, and sending the drone into the flames at Fire on research station for a drone schematic instead of a coin flip with a crew member's life.
  • Slug ×2A lookout while you strip the wreck at Disabled Rock ship — both of its outcomes pay and neither ends in a fight — and a read on what is really down there at Single life form on moon.

What you can leave with

  • AugmentDamaged Stasis Pod at Dense asteroid field distress — the only named item the whole pool hands out, and it sits in the one line the map can starve.
  • CrewRockmen two ways: talked round at Rock atheists, where Improved Sensors makes it certain and the plain appeal is one outcome in three, or bought at Slaver (friendly).
  • CrewSlaver (friendly) is the one beacon that can produce a Mantis, a Rockman or an Engi; Crew hiring station sells outright; Single life form on moon can hand you a human off a moon with a level already in something.
  • Crew26 of the 50 events in the pool can add someone — over half the sector, against five that can take someone away.
  • ReactorA free reactor bar is one of three follow-ups at Rock and Slug standoff if you settle the Rock ship's debt for 10–15 scrap; another of the three sells you the same upgrade at the same price.
  • WeaponA high-tier weapon out of Unknown disease on mining colony — but only with an upgraded Medbay and the Engi Med-bot Dispersal to spread the cure.

What can bite

  • CrewFive events can kill a crew member outright, four of them inside the distress line: Giant alien spiders, Fire on research station, Unknown disease on mining colony and Single life form on moon. The fifth is Rock live mine. That line is filled early, so the risk arrives early too.
  • EnvironmentFive of the eight hostile and boarding events open inside a hazard — three asteroid fields, two suns — and a hazard freezes the ship menu while you sit in it: no upgrades, no cargo swaps, no crew reassignment until you jump. At the suns, holding shields up keeps a flare to one or two fires instead of three to six.
  • BoardersFour events put someone aboard. Three are Rockmen, up to three of them at Rock fight with boarders and at Boarders: Rockmen near sun; Encrypted federation signal sends two to three humans.
  • HullCapture the ship is a job to take a ship intact. Destroy the target instead and the wreck costs you 13 hull, a random system, and one room hit with every effect at once.
  • AmmoRock live mine chews through the hull while you decide: a missile detonation costs 3 hull, a beam drone costs a drone part, and defusing it by hand can drop into a red-wire/blue-wire flip where the wrong end kills the volunteer, takes 5 hull and opens a breach. Engines 5 backs you out of the whole thing for nothing.

Reading the map here

  • DistressRock is the exception to the note above: the distress line is filled before the neutral pool, not after, so the extra marker that Dense asteroid field distress carries comes from the very bottom of the table and is the first one a full map loses. Two events in the distress line show no marker at all, so an ordinary-looking beacon can still be one of them.
  • ScannersLong-Ranged Scanners split the marked beacons exactly one way here: a possible-ship reading means Friendly ship out of fuel, Escort civilians FTL haywire or Pirate ship distress trap — two payouts and one ambush. Every other distress beacon in the sector reports as a plain unvisited location.
  • StoresTwo, fixed, no roll, and first in the fill order — the shopping is the one guarantee this sector makes. Anything beyond it is an event opening a shop, and two of the three that can are in the 0–1 quest line.
  • No cloud coverThe sector allocates no nebula line, so no purple clouds are drawn on the map: sensors work at every beacon, no jump you make slows the pursuit, and every hazard here is a sun or an asteroid field you can see coming with scanners.

Generation rules per the community's reverse-engineering of the map generator; the game files state none of them.

minSector in the files is zero-indexed, so the "earliest sector" chip is that value plus one — the number a player counts from the start of a run. The community wiki states the same offset.

The 24-beacon ceiling follows from the 6×4 grid; the 19-beacon floor is stated by the community wiki alone and is not derivable from anything else here, so the at-risk marks use the ceiling.

"Unique" is the files' own unique="true". Whether that means once per sector or once per run is not stated in any file here; the wiki's better bet is once per sector.

The shipped event lists carry no weights, so no beacon here has stated odds. A pool entry listed twice is counted as ×2 and marked.
